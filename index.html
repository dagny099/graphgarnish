How an answer is found

The show publishes no transcripts, so the last 20 minutes of each episode are transcribed by machine. The hosts ask all three questions in one breath, and the guest answers them in turn:

“To wrap up, three questions. What's your advice…? Who should we invite next? And what resources do you follow?” · “Okay, so advice, I would say… As far as people, who haven't you had on…?”

The host's words find the questions. The guest's own turns of phrase mark where each answer starts and stops. When the pipeline can't tell one answer from the next, it leaves that episode blank rather than guess.

Answers are short excerpts, transcribed by machine from the episode audio, so words and names can be misheard. Each one links to its moment in the audio, so you can hear exactly what was said.

The same pipeline also reads the Data Engineering Podcast, which asks every guest about the biggest gap in data tooling today: 95 answers from its 120 most recent episodes.

How it works

No spreadsheets and no uploads. Everything is rebuilt from the show's public feed by scripts in this repository, on a schedule.

The guest network

  1. Read the whole feedEvery page of the RSS feed, so the back catalogue is never silently cut off.
  2. Extract and checkGuests, organisations and topics come out of titles and descriptions. Every link records whether it was curated, corrected by hand, or inferred.
  3. Refresh weeklyA scheduled job rebuilds the network every Monday and publishes it here, refusing any rebuild that would lose episodes.

The answers

  1. Get the wordsA published transcript when the show has one; otherwise speech-to-text on the last 20 minutes of each episode, run across 16 machines at once.
  2. Find and cutLocate the question by the host's phrasing, then cut the guest's answer out of the following lines.
  3. ConnectLink answers to the people, things and ideas they name, and to answers worded alike. Short excerpts are published; full text stays private.

Point it at your own podcast

Any show that ends with a recurring question can get the same treatment. A podcast is one small settings file: its feed, its hosts, and the questions they always ask. A new question is one more entry in that file.

To try an idea without writing a file, give the show's name and the question. The feed is found by name, and a sample of episodes spread across the years shows whether the question can be found before running on all of them.

Already have a network of your own? The guest-network explorer loads any network file in the same format.

# one question, any show, eight episodes as a trial
python3 tools/qa_graph.py --podcast "Data Engineering Podcast" \
    --ask "What is the biggest gap in the tooling?" \
    --cue "biggest gap in the tooling" --sample 8 --report

# or a config file per show: podcasts/<show>.json
python3 tools/qa_graph.py podcasts/catalog-and-cocktails.json
